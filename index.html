Pre-Op Evaluation
Companion Guide

Template Overview

A preoperative evaluation is among the most valuable contributions an internist makes to a patient's surgical care. Every operation carries intrinsic risk alongside modifiable risk, and our role is to identify and address the latter, targeting conditions such as uncontrolled hypertension or diabetes that meaningfully increase perioperative danger. A thorough evaluation also provides an individualized estimate of total surgical risk, which is essential for informed consent and shared decision making. The goal is not to order every available test, but to ask the right questions, act on findings that matter, and communicate risk clearly so that patients and surgeons can proceed with confidence.

To get started, add the Smart Phrases below to your note. Then use this guide to help you complete the evaluation.

Subjective (S)

HPI

State the planned surgery, indication, date if known, and surgeon.

"61M with HTN, HLD, and RA presenting for pre-op evaluation prior to bunionectomy + hammertoe repair scheduled with Dr. [Name] indicated due to chronic pain limiting ambulation."
PMHx

List all significant medical problems. Omit minor or resolved issues. For major conditions, include key details such as:

  • Diabetes → most recent A1c
  • CHF → most recent EF
  • CKD → baseline creatinine
  • CAD → stent type, date
  • OSA → CPAP use, compliance
Medications

Include OTC medications and supplements. Flag these for perioperative management decisions:

Cardiovascular

  • Anticoagulants (warfarin, DOACs, heparin)
  • Antiplatelet agents (aspirin, clopidogrel)
  • Beta-blockers
  • ACEi / ARBs
  • Diuretics
  • Clonidine
  • Statins
  • Calcium channel blockers

Endocrine / Other

  • Insulin (type, dose, timing)
  • Metformin
  • SGLT-2 inhibitors
  • GLP-1 agonists (injectable vs. oral)
  • Steroids (dose and duration)
  • SSRI / SNRI / TCAs
  • Supplements: ginkgo biloba, garlic, high-dose vitamin E, turmeric
PSH

Prior surgeries with attention to anesthesia intolerance or reactions, prior surgical complications, difficult airway history, and h/o malignant hyperthermia.

Allergies

Medications and anesthetic agents. Document the type of reaction for each.

Social History

Tobacco (ppd, quit date or active), EtOH (frequency/quantity), illicit drugs, and — critically — functional status.

Functional Status: "Can you climb 2 flights of stairs or walk 2 blocks at a brisk pace without stopping?" If yes → ≥4 METs. If no → consider further workup. See METs section.
Family History

Focus on bleeding disorders, clotting disorders, and anesthesia problems (malignant hyperthermia).

Objective (O)

Vital Signs

Be sure to document temp, HR, BP, RR, weight, and BMI. Take note of uncontrolled hypertension, tachycardia, or low O₂ saturation.

Physical Exam

Prioritize the cardiopulmonary exam. Use the CLUE exam if indicated.

AreaWhat to Assess
Neck (JVP)Elevated JVP → volume overload / CHF
LungsWheezes, rhonchi, crackles (rales → pulmonary edema)
CardiovascularRate, rhythm, murmurs, S3/S4
AbdomenDistension, ascites (especially in liver disease), tenderness
ExtremitiesBilateral leg edema → CHF or venous insufficiency
Recent Data

List relevant results: CBC, CMP, A1c, EKG, echo, CXR, ect. See Further testing section for decision support on ordering additional testing or workup.

Assessment & Plan (A/P)

The A/P synthesizes everything. Every item below must be addressed.

Required Elements

  1. Patient risk — low, moderate, or high (based on active conditions and RCRI score)
  2. Surgery risk — inherent procedure risk (low / intermediate / high)
  3. RCRI — score and estimated 30-day MACE risk percentage
  4. Further testing — what is ordered, and why (or why no workup is needed)
  5. Medication instructions — hold/continue/adjust; inform patient AND surgeon
  6. Antibiotic prophylaxis — indicated or not, with regimen if applicable
  7. Caprini VTE score — score, risk tier, prophylaxis recommendation
  8. Transfusion consent — documented (or patient's decline documented)
  9. Code status — confirmed and documented
End the A/P with a clear declarative statement: "Patient is medically optimized for surgery" — or document why they are not, and what action is needed.

Sample Language

Low-risk patient, low-risk surgery

"Low risk for a low-risk procedure. No active high-risk cardiac conditions. RCRI 0 — estimated 30-day MACE risk <1%. Functional status ≥4 METs. No further cardiac workup indicated. Patient is medically optimized for surgery."

Elevated-risk patient

"Moderate risk for an intermediate-risk procedure. RCRI 2 — estimated 30-day MACE risk ~2.5%. Functional status <4 METs. Further workup with stress testing ordered given elevated MACE risk and poor functional status."

Functional Status / METs

Functional status is one of the strongest predictors of cardiovascular readiness for surgery. Patients who cannot perform at least 4 METs at baseline need further cardiac evaluation considered.

The threshold is 4 METs. Ask: "Can you climb 2 flights of stairs or walk 2 blocks on flat ground at a brisk pace without stopping?" Yes → ≥4 METs.

Documentation Language

Adequate: "Patient fully functional at baseline. Able to climb 2 flights of stairs and walk several blocks without symptoms. Functional status ≥4 METs."
Limited: "Patient with limited functional status. Unable to climb a full flight of stairs due to dyspnea. Functional status estimated 2-3 METs. Further cardiac evaluation warranted."

If functional status is unknown or unreliable, treat as <4 METs and apply workup criteria accordingly.

Surgery Risk Classification

Required element of every pre-op eval. Risk is based on expected physiologic disruption, hemodynamic impact, and blood loss potential.

Emergent/urgent surgery is an independent risk factor. Document and communicate this explicitly regardless of other risk factors.
Cardiac workup is required for all patients undergoing liver transplant or aortic surgery, regardless of other risk factors.
Low Risk
Minimal physiologic disruption. No additional cardiac workup typically needed without active cardiac conditions.
Examples: GI endoscopy, cataract surgery, minor dental procedures, breast biopsy, dermatologic surgery
Intermediate Risk
Moderate physiologic impact. Cardiac workup may be needed if risk factors are present.
Examples: Most orthopedic surgeries (hip, knee, spine), intraabdominal, head and neck, prostate, pulmonary resection
High Risk
Significant hemodynamic disruption, major blood loss risk. Cardiac workup warranted for most patients with risk factors.
Examples: Intrathoracic, major intraperitoneal, infra-inguinal vascular, aortic surgery

RCRI Calculator

The Revised Cardiac Risk Index (RCRI) estimates 30-day risk of a major adverse cardiac event (MACE = MI, cardiac arrest, or cardiac death) for noncardiac surgery. Recommended by 2024 AHA/ACC guidelines.

RCRI — Check all that apply
RCRI Score: 0 — Estimated 30-day MACE risk: <1%
RCRI ScoreEstimated 30-day MACE RiskInterpretation

Alternatives: MICA (Gupta) on MDCalc — predicts MI or cardiac arrest at 30 days. NSQIP (ACS) — comprehensive but time-consuming. Both are acceptable.

Further Testing Decision Support

General principle: If the surgeon requests a test, order it (unless unethical). Otherwise, order based on clinical indication — not reflexively. No additional workup is typically needed for low-risk surgery with RCRI <1% and ≥4 METs.

Test-Specific Indications

EKG

Indicated when: Known cardiac disease (CAD, PVD, stroke, arrhythmia, cardiomyopathy, CHF); active cardiac symptoms; significant cardiac risk factors.

A positive EKG finding (including Q waves) → cardiology workup or consultation.

Routine EKG in asymptomatic low-risk patients is generally not recommended.

Echocardiogram (TTE)

Indicated when: Unexplained dyspnea on exertion without known CV diagnosis; new or uncharacterized murmur; active or decompensated CHF; valve disease with symptoms or worse than mild severity; no echo within 1 year in significant valve disease; abnormal CLUE exam.

Laboratory Studies

Indicated when: Known or suspected anemia (CBC); risk for bleeding or coagulopathy; liver disease (LFTs, coags, CBC for platelets); renal disease (BMP/creatinine); diabetes — A1c must be within 3 months of surgery.

Most surgeons routinely request CBC and BMP.

Chest X-Ray (CXR)

Indicated when: Abnormal lung exam (rales, rhonchi, possible pleural effusion); hypoxia; active pulmonary symptoms; active lung disease; OSA with pulmonary concerns or pulmonary HTN.

Spirometry / PFTs

Indicated when: Active lung disease that is symptomatic or insufficiently treated. Helps characterize severity and guides optimization before surgery.

Cardiac Stress Testing / CT Coronary Angiography

Consider when: New onset angina, exertional chest pain, or dyspnea on exertion without known CV diagnosis; or elevated cardiovascular risk (RCRI ≥2) + intermediate/high-risk surgery + poor or unknown functional status — only if a positive result would change management.

Mandatory for all patients undergoing liver transplant or aortic surgery.

Do not order if the result will not change management.

Document instructions explicitly in the A/P for patient and surgeon. Default is to hold the morning of surgery unless specified below.

Antibiotic Prophylaxis

IE prophylaxis is indicated for high-risk cardiac conditions undergoing procedures likely to cause bacteremia. Most routine surgical procedures do not require IE prophylaxis.

Cardiac Conditions Requiring Prophylaxis

  • Prosthetic cardiac valves (mechanical or bioprosthetic)
  • Prior infective endocarditis
  • Unrepaired cyanotic congenital heart disease (CHD)
  • CHD repaired with prosthetic material within the past 6 months
  • Repaired CHD with residual defects adjacent to prosthetic material
  • Cardiac transplant recipients with cardiac valvulopathy

Procedures Warranting Prophylaxis

  • Dental procedures involving manipulation of gingival tissue or perforation of oral mucosa
  • Respiratory tract procedures involving incision of respiratory mucosa
  • GI/GU procedures — only if active GI/GU infection at time of procedure
Document in A/P: "Antibiotic prophylaxis: indicated due to [condition] — [regimen]" OR "Antibiotic prophylaxis: not indicated."

The "Killer 6" — Active High-Risk Cardiac Conditions

If any of these are present, postpone elective surgery. These must be stabilized before proceeding.
Unstable Angina

Angina at rest, new onset angina, or crescendo angina (increasing frequency or severity). Stable exertional angina that is predictable and unchanged is NOT in this category.

  • Refer to cardiology urgently
  • Cardiac catheterization likely needed to evaluate coronary anatomy
MI Within 3 Months (Without Revascularization)

High risk for recurrent infarction perioperatively.

  • Delay surgery ≥3 months from MI if non-emergent
  • Consult cardiology regarding revascularization status
  • If urgently needed, ensure cardiologic involvement in perioperative management
Severe Valvular Disease or Severe Pulmonary HTN

Includes severe AS, severe MS, or severe pulmonary arterial hypertension.

  • Refer to cardiology — consider valve intervention before elective surgery
  • Echo within 1 year required for known valve disease; sooner if symptoms or new murmur
  • Severe pulmonary HTN: consult pulmonology/cardiology; high risk for right heart failure perioperatively
Severe Dysrhythmia

High-degree AV block, symptomatic bradycardia, uncontrolled ventricular or supraventricular tachyarrhythmias, or new malignant arrhythmia. Rate-controlled atrial fibrillation is NOT in this category.

  • Refer to cardiology for rhythm control or pacemaker evaluation
  • EKG required; Holter monitor may be indicated
Decompensated CHF

Active fluid overload, dyspnea at rest or with minimal exertion, or new/worsening CHF. Compensated CHF (clinically stable, euvolemic) is NOT in this category.

  • Optimize volume status with diuresis as needed
  • Cardiology consultation
  • Echo to assess EF and valve function if not recently done
  • Once compensated and at dry weight → can reconsider surgical clearance
Recent PCI — Angioplasty ≤2 wk · BMS ≤1 mo · DES ≤3 mo

Very high risk of in-stent thrombosis if DAPT is interrupted within these windows.

  • Angioplasty only: delay ≥2 weeks
  • Bare metal stent (BMS): delay ≥1 month
  • Drug-eluting stent (DES): delay ≥3 months (ideally 6 months; minimum 3 months if critical)
  • Consult cardiology regarding antiplatelet management and timing

Condition-Specific Recommendations

Cardiac Device — AICD
  • AICD must be turned OFF by cardiology before the procedure, and turned ON again (and interrogated) after
  • Coordinate with electrophysiology / device clinic well in advance
  • Confirm device type, settings, and last interrogation date
Cardiac Device — Pacemaker
  • Must be set to a fixed rate by cardiology before surgery to prevent inhibition from electrocautery
  • Reset to original programming after the procedure
  • Determine pacemaker dependency — if dependent, more stringent intraoperative precautions apply
CVD / High Cardiac Risk — Postoperative Monitoring

For patients with CVD or age >65 with multiple risk factors undergoing high-risk surgery:

  • Daily EKG and troponins ×48 hours postoperatively
  • Telemetry ×72 hours postoperatively
  • Include in A/P and communicate to surgical team
Coronary Stents — On DAPT
  • Do NOT stop DAPT without cardiology guidance — risk of catastrophic in-stent thrombosis
  • Consult cardiology for all patients on DAPT for CAD before any antiplatelet decisions
  • Try to delay surgery for 6 months after DES (at least 3mos if critical), 1 month after BMS. If surgery cannot wait, discuss risk-benefit with cardiology and surgery together
OSA (Obstructive Sleep Apnea)
  • Screen with ROS: snoring, witnessed apneas, daytime sleepiness
  • If undiagnosed and concern is high → consider sleep study before elective surgery
  • Continue home CPAP perioperatively
  • Continue home O₂ if prescribed
  • Moderate or severe OSA: explicitly document increased risk for perioperative respiratory complications in the A/P
Diabetes Mellitus
  • A1c must be within 3 months of surgery — document the value in the note
  • Consider endocrinology consult if: poorly controlled DM, or any type 1 DM
  • Adjust oral medications per medication table (see Medication Management)
  • Inform both patient AND surgeon of all diabetes medication changes
  • Reference: UpToDate — "Perioperative glycemic management in adults with diabetes mellitus"
Chronic Liver Disease / Cirrhosis

Check Original MELD score (pre-2016 version, without sodium) on MDCalc:

MELD Score3-Month Mortality RiskRecommendation
<10Acceptable for most surgeriesProceed with caution
10–15Elevated — acceptable with precautionsProceed with careful planning
>15HighAvoid elective surgery
  • Order LFTs, coags (PT/INR), and CBC (platelets) for all patients with chronic liver disease
  • Treat coagulopathy and ascites before surgery
  • Portal Is a risk factor for surgical complications → hepatology consultation
  • Abdominal and thoracic surgeries carry much higher risk in liver disease
  • Acute hepatitis (especially alcoholic) is a contraindication to surgery
Pulmonary Disease (COPD, Pulmonary HTN, Active Lung Disease)
  • Consider CXR for all patients with lung disease, OSA, pulmonary HTN, or significant smoking history
  • Check spirometry if symptomatic or insufficiently treated
  • Optimize COPD management (bronchodilators, steroids if indicated)
  • Pulmonary HTN: pulmonology or cardiology consult; high perioperative risk
  • Active smoking: counsel on cessation — stopping even a few weeks before surgery reduces complications
Bleeding / Coagulation Disorder
  • Obtain PT/INR, PTT, platelet count
  • Bleeding history without diagnosis → consider hematology consult
  • Anticoagulated patients: identify hold/restart dates and bridging strategy; document for patient and surgeon
  • Transfusion consent must be documented for all patients
Active or Recent Infection
  • Active infection (fevers, chills, currently on antibiotics): delay elective surgery
  • Emergent/urgent surgery with active infection: proceed but notify surgical and anesthesia teams; document clinical context
